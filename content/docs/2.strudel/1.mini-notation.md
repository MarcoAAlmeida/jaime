# Mini-notation

The string you pass to `s()`, `note()`, or `n()` is its own tiny
language. A space-separated list is a sequence spread evenly across the
cycle:

```strudel
s("bd hh sd hh")
```

## Rests and repeats

- `~` is a rest — a step with nothing in it.
- `x*3` plays `x` three times in that step's slot (subdivide).
- `x/2` plays `x` once every two cycles (slow it down).
- `x!3` repeats `x` as three separate steps (stretch).
- `x@3` gives `x` three steps' worth of length (weight).

```strudel
s("bd*2 ~ sd ~, hh!4")
```

## Grouping

- `[a b]` is a sub-sequence — it packs into one step, so it plays twice
  as fast here:

  ```strudel
  s("bd [hh hh] sd [hh hh hh]")
  ```

- `<a b c>` plays one item per cycle, cycling through — alternation:

  ```strudel
  note("<c e g>").s("triangle")
  ```

- `a , b` stacks — both play at once (polyphony / layering):

  ```strudel
  s("bd*4, hh*8, ~ cp")
  ```

## Euclidean rhythms

`x(k,n)` distributes `k` hits as evenly as possible across `n` steps —
the backbone of a lot of world and electronic rhythm:

```strudel
s("bd(3,8), hh(5,8)")
```

Add a third number to rotate the pattern's starting point: `x(3,8,2)`.

See these ideas in practice: filter the
[pattern library](/app/patterns) by the `euclidean` tag.
